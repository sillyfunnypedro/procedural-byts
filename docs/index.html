Procedural Byts

A computer science curriculum in heavy metal, 1970 to now

AlbumsMaking ofThe bandTriviaCurriculumUsing with Suno

Procedural Byts are a fictional band who have released a record every three to five years since 1970. Each album covers one area of the ACM/IEEE-CS/AAAI CS2023 computer science curriculum in the metal style of its decade, from 1970 proto-metal to a 2024 quantum reunion. Every song teaches one concept, and the solos act it out. There are 18 albums and 218 songs, covering all seventeen CS2023 knowledge areas plus quantum computing. The duck has been there the whole time.

The Band

  • NybleLead vocals, bass guitar1970 to nowFour bits. Half a byte, one hex digit, and the voice of the band on every album.
  • BytLead guitar1970 to 1988, and 2024 in superpositionOne bit. Flipped mid-solo in 1988; the band blames a cosmic ray. The band is named after him.
  • BytesDrums, backing vocals1970 to nowEight bits and eight flip-flops. Counts every loop out loud, raps, screams, growls, and was once told he could not be scaled up.
  • WyrdSynthesizers, keytar, organ, turntables, piano1985 to nowThirty-two bits. Answered a magazine ad that said “must align on word boundaries.” Remembers the exact bar Byt stopped on.
  • QwordLead guitar1988 to nowSixty-four bits. The sixty-fourth guitarist to audition. Went without guitar solos from 1992 to 2003 and has been making up for it since. Shares lead guitar with Byt on the 2024 reunion.
  • NullRubber duck, technical advisorWas there firstNever returns anything. Returned exactly one value, ever, at the end of Q.E.D. in 1989.

Trivia

  • 1970The band met in a university basement lab at midnight, each explaining their bugs out loud to the same rubber duck. By sunrise they had fixed each other’s bugs, and the duck had said nothing.
  • 1970The name was a typo. Nyble meant to write “Procedural Bits” on the first flyer. Byt filed it as a bug; Nyble closed it as “working as intended.”
  • 1970At the first gig, Bytes counted in “zero, one, two, three” and the others came in on four. The whole set ran one beat apart. Off-by-one has been the band’s signature ever since.
  • 1973The second album was recorded inside a mainframe cabinet “for the reverb.” Null sat on the halt switch during one take and stopped the machine; the take is on the record.
  • 1976The Kernel Panic sessions deadlocked for three weeks. It ended when someone moved the duck, which had been sitting on the only lyric sheet.
  • 1976Byt once forked himself to play rhythm and lead at the same time. The child process ate everyone’s sandwiches until Nyble reaped it.
  • 1979Side B of Syntax Error has no guitar. Byt refused to play songs with no side effects.
  • 1982Packet Storm was recorded by mail. Bytes’s reels arrived out of order and were reassembled by sequence number. One never arrived. Null traveled in every box as packing material.
  • 1985The tour bus was named the Linked List. It drove past Pittsburgh and couldn’t go back, because it had no back pointer.
  • 1985In Cleveland, Bytes read the setlist as a stack and the band opened with the encore.
  • 1985On the live album, Nyble still introduces the band by its old name, Bytes, Nybles and Byts. The recordings were kept as they were.
  • 1988Byt flipped, mid-solo. He left one line on the bottom of the duck: “If n, then n plus one.” No base case.
  • 1989The base case turned out to be Hello, World, the first song they ever played. At the end of the proof, Null quacked. It is the only value the duck has ever returned.
  • 1992Every member kept their own copy of the setlist, and no two matched. Bytes’s still listed songs from 1979.
  • 1992Asked whether it was equal to itself, Null said nothing. In SQL, that is the correct answer.
  • 1995Every code review comment Bytes wrote said “louder.” The merge took twice as long as the recording.
  • 1998A fan got backstage by saying “I’m with the duck.” Backstage passes now need two factors. The security question is “what does the duck say?” The answer is nothing.
  • 1998Bytes’s password was “drums,” then “drums1,” then “Drums1!”
  • 2001The title track of Race Condition came out two different ways depending on whose tape machine started first. Both versions are on the album; one is hidden.
  • 2001With four members, every vote tied two to two, so Null became the fifth voter. The duck abstains every time and has never lost an election.
  • 2004Wyrd built the band’s first 3D music video by hand and ran out of time before modeling the duck, which ended up with twelve triangles. The low-poly duck is now the band’s logo.
  • 2007The first pressing of User Error came in packaging nobody could open. The second pressing says “pull here.”
  • 2010The version of Cross-Platform that runs on a smart fridge, in 64 kilobytes, is the most-played version. In the app’s game, Null has a position, a sprite and a velocity, but no sound component.
  • 2013The band toured as four virtual machines on one very loud server, until its fan failed on the first night and took all four down at once.
  • 2013Null has had perfect uptime since 1970, mostly by not doing anything.
  • 2016The band read its 1970 record contract for the first time. Page eighty-seven gave the label the rights to “any mascot, waterfowl or technical advisor.” They got the duck back and released its likeness under Creative Commons.
  • 2016Duty of Care, about the Therac-25, is the only Procedural Byts song with no jokes in it.
  • 2019The label trained an AI on every song the band had recorded and released it as Procedural Bots. It topped the charts for a week, until fans noticed every duck had five legs.
  • 2019Procedural Bots was always a beat early, because its training data included Bytes’s practice tapes.
  • 2024Byt returned as a qubit: both in the band and not, until someone checks. Wyrd remembered the exact bar he stopped on in 1988, and Byt picked up on the next one.
  • 2024The last song on the last album stops before its final note. Whether Byt played it, and whether the duck quacked a second time, has never been measured.

Using with Suno

Every album page has a Copy complete Suno prompt button for each song, plus each Suno field on its own: title, style of music, exclude styles and lyrics. In Suno, switch on Custom mode and paste each field into its box.

Each style prompt describes the lineup for that era, so the band keeps a consistent sound: female lead vocals on bass guitar, the lead guitarist, the keyboard player once Wyrd joins, and the drummer on backing vocals. Section tags in the lyrics say who sings what, and words in parentheses are sung as backing vocals.

Lyrics, lessons and prompts are also in the repository as plain Markdown, one file per song.
