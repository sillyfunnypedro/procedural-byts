Merge Conflict is the 1995 album from Procedural Byts: twelve tracks of industrial metal about how software actually gets built, by people, together. Nyble sings lead and plays bass, Qword plays lead guitar, Wyrd plugs the synths back in alongside a rack of samplers, and Bytes plays drums over machine loops and sings backup. Requirements, process, design, patterns, testing, code review, refactoring, technical debt, estimation and version control. The first album the band made with a written plan. The Band

The four-piece, plugged back in. Nyble sings lead on every song and plays bass guitar. Qword plays lead guitar. Wyrd plays synthesizers and samplers. Bytes plays drums over machine loops and sings backup.

Nyble

Lead vocals, bass guitar

Four bits, and the author of the band’s first requirements document. Nyble sings lead on every song and plays bass, and every song on this album had to trace back to a line she wrote.

Qword

Lead guitar

Sixty-four bits, and allowed to solo again, as long as the solo passes review. Qword’s branch had the most changes on the album and the most conflicts.

Wyrd

Synthesizers, samplers

Thirty-two bits, back on synthesizers after the organ years, plus a rack of samplers. Wyrd turned the studio photocopier into a drum loop and has never apologized.

Bytes

Drums, backing vocals

Eight bits, playing live drums over machine loops and singing backup. Every code review comment he wrote said “louder.” Nobody has been able to argue with it.

Null

Rubber duck, technical advisor

Reviewer of last resort. Approved every change on the album by not objecting.

Four Branches

For the first time in twenty-five years, the band wrote down what they were going to make before they made it. Nyble sent a survey to the fan club asking what people wanted from the next album. The top four answers were louder, longer, angrier and more duck.

Industrial was in, so Wyrd plugged the synthesizers back in and added a rack of samplers. Wyrd recorded the studio’s air conditioner, the photocopier and Bytes dropping his sticks, and turned all three into rhythm tracks.

They tried the waterfall first: a month of requirements, a month of design, then recording, with testing saved for the end. At the end, they discovered the whole album was in the wrong key, and went back to the top.

So each member took their own branch: one copy of the songs each, changed independently for six weeks. The recording took two months. The merge took four. Nyble and Qword had both rewritten the chorus of Merge Conflict on the same night, in completely different ways, and you can hear both on the record.

Every part had to pass code review by another member before it went on the master tape. Qword’s reviews were long and thoughtful. Wyrd’s were precise. Every review Bytes wrote said “louder.” When two members couldn’t agree, they explained their sides to Null, and by the time they finished explaining, one of them usually realized they were wrong.

The album came in eleven weeks late. Bytes suggested bringing in session players to catch up. Nyble pointed out that adding people to a late project makes it later. They did it anyway, and proved her right.
