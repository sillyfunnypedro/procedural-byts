## The lesson

In a man-in-the-middle attack, an attacker secretly sits between two parties, relaying and possibly changing everything they say, for example by handing each side the attacker’s own public key. The defense is to authenticate keys: certificates signed by trusted certificate authorities bind a public key to a real identity, as SSL (today TLS) does for websites. Alice, Bob and Mallory are cryptography’s classic cast.
