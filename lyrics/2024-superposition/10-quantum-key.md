## The lesson

Quantum key distribution, like the BB84 protocol from 1984, sends photons prepared in randomly chosen ways. The receiver measures each one in a randomly chosen way too, then both sides compare their choices in public and keep only the results where they matched, giving them a shared secret key. Because measuring a quantum state disturbs it, and it can’t be copied, an eavesdropper leaves errors behind that the two sides can detect.
