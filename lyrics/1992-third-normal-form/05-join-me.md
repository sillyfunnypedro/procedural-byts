## The lesson

A join combines rows from two tables that match on a condition, usually a foreign key equal to a primary key. An inner join keeps only the rows that match on both sides. A left outer join keeps every row from the left table and fills in NULL where there’s no match. Leave out the condition and you get the cross product: every row paired with every other row.
